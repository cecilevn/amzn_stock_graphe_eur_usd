How to read this

  • Both lines track the same company — the gap between them is entirely currency. It peaked in April 2008 (€1 = $1.60, hurting European holders) and narrowed by late 2000 (€1 = $0.83, favoring them).
  • The euro line isn't a European investor's actual net return — no FX fees, no taxes, no entry-timing effect. It isolates the mechanical impact of converting the price every day.
  • Prices adjusted for stock splits (2:1 in 1998, 3:1 and 2:1 in 1999, 20:1 in June 2022). Amazon has never paid a dividend, so price and total return are the same.
